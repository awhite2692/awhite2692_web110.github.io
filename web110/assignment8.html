Web Browser Safety and Privacy: This activity helped me understand the importance of keeping a web browser secure and managing the privacy settings that affect my online activity. I learned that browsers include several features that can help protect users, including security settings, private browsing, cookie management, saved form information, and tools for checking whether a website uses a secure connection. Some of this material reinforced what I had previously learned in Harper College's CompTIA Tech+ and CompTIA Network+ courses, particularly the differences between HTTP and HTTPS and the role of digital certificates. HTTP is used to transfer information between a browser and a website, while HTTPS uses encryption to help protect that information while it is being transmitted. Digital certificates also help verify a website's identity and establish a secure connection. I primarily use Mozilla Firefox on my Mac, so understanding Firefox's privacy and security settings is especially relevant to me. Clearing the browser cache, reviewing cookies, and managing browsing history can help me control what information is stored on my computer. Private browsing can also be useful when I do not want a browsing session to save its history and cookies in the usual way, although it does not make me completely anonymous online. I can apply this knowledge by regularly checking for browser updates, reviewing extensions, managing saved personal information, and paying attention to website security indicators and certificate warnings. These practices can help me protect my personal information and make more informed decisions when browsing websites or entering sensitive information online. Anti-Malware Software and Threat Detection: The anti-malware activity connected to security practices I already use on my Mac. I regularly use CleanMyMac X, which includes a malware removal feature, to help identify and address potential threats on my computer. I also use a VPN when downloading certain files. This activity helped me better understand that different security tools serve different purposes. Anti-malware software is designed to detect and remove malicious software, while a VPN encrypts network traffic between my device and the VPN service and can help protect the privacy of that connection. However, a VPN does not automatically make downloaded files safe or prevent all malware infections, so it is still important to be careful about the sources of files. I also learned about the EICAR test file, which is designed to test antivirus detection without using actual malware. It provides a way to check whether security software recognizes a known test signature. I can apply this knowledge by keeping CleanMyMac X and my operating system updated, being cautious about downloading files from unfamiliar websites, and paying attention to security alerts. I also understand that no security application can guarantee complete protection. Combining security software with careful browsing and downloading habits is important for protecting my personal files and information. Password Managers, Multi-Factor Authentication, and Identity Protection: This activity reinforced the importance of protecting online accounts and personal information through strong passwords, multi-factor authentication, and other security measures. Password managers can generate and store strong, unique passwords, reducing the need to remember a different password for every website or service. Multi-factor authentication adds another layer of protection by requiring an additional form of verification beyond a password, such as an authenticator app or security key. I already use Microsoft Authenticator to access Harper College and certain email accounts, and most, if not all, of my financial accounts require multi-factor authentication when I sign in through their apps or websites. I have also experienced concerns about identity theft firsthand. In the past, Capital One alerted me that some of my personal information had been found on the dark web, raising concerns about potential identity theft. I responded by placing a freeze on my credit and reporting the issue. This experience showed me that protecting my information involves more than securing my computer or choosing strong passwords. Personal information exposed through data breaches can create risks even when I take precautions with my own devices and accounts. I can apply what I learned by continuing to use multi-factor authentication, maintaining unique passwords, and monitoring alerts from my financial institutions. I can also keep credit freezes in place as appropriate to help prevent someone from opening new credit accounts in my name. This activity reinforced the importance of being proactive about digital security and responding quickly when I receive warnings that my personal information may have been exposed.
