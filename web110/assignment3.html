Compare Search Engines: This activity focused on comparing Google, Bing, Yahoo, and Ask and examining the differences in their user interfaces and search results. I learned that different search engines can present information differently, even when using the same search terms. I preferred Google’s user interface because its dark mode is easier on my eyes, and I felt that its colors and visual elements were larger and stood out more clearly. However, I found Bing provided the most useful results for this particular search because it presented the most relevant and useful figures and percentages on the initial results page. Bing and Yahoo also appeared similar in their overall interfaces. I also learned that Ask.com is no longer operating as a search engine as of May 1, 2026. When researching search engine market share, I found that Google has by far the highest worldwide market share, at roughly 91% in 2026. This activity reinforced for me that the most popular search engine is not necessarily the one that will provide the most useful results for every search. This knowledge is applicable to my current Web Design and Interactive Media coursework because it helps me understand how users evaluate and interact with websites and online information. It also reinforces the importance of usability, visual presentation, and relevant information when designing websites and other digital experiences. Learn About Search Engine Optimization (SEO): This activity introduced me to search engine optimization and how search engines discover and index web pages. I learned about web crawlers, also known as spiders, and how they systematically visit web pages, follow links, and collect information that search engines can use to index content. I found it interesting to learn that simply creating and publishing a web page does not guarantee that it will immediately appear in search results. Search engines have to discover and crawl a page before it can be indexed and potentially appear in search results. I also learned that using search options to narrow results can make research more effective. For example, limiting results to government websites using the .gov domain can help when looking for authoritative government information. I can apply this knowledge to my current and future work with virtual instructor-led training (VILT) and learning technology. Although SEO is not directly used to make a Zoom or Microsoft Teams meeting appear in search results, the principles of organization, discoverability, and effective searching can apply to training content and resources surrounding those platforms. Training videos, recordings, transcripts, FAQs, and course materials can be organized with clear titles, descriptions, headings, and other metadata so learners can more easily find the information they need. These skills will be useful in digital learning and instructional technology, where both creating accessible content and helping learners locate reliable information are important parts of the learning experience. Learn About Artificial Intelligence (AI): This activity reinforced and expanded on concepts I previously learned in Harper College’s Careers in AI and Cloud Computing course, which I completed during the spring semester. Reviewing the differences between artificial intelligence and machine learning reinforced my understanding of how these technologies work, while the discussion of the risks and shortcomings of large language models connected directly to topics I had previously studied. In particular, I learned about AI hallucinations and bias and the importance of maintaining critical thinking when using AI-generated information. Because AI and machine learning are developed and trained using human-generated data and input, determining fact from fiction is an ongoing challenge. I believe vigilance and rigorous ethical standards are vital to the continued development of AI and to addressing problems such as false or misleading information, privacy violations, and harmful biases that can contribute to inaccurate results. I am also concerned that becoming too dependent on AI could cause people to accept generated answers without questioning their accuracy or considering different perspectives. The activity reinforced discussions from my previous course about the fear that AI and robotics will replace people in the workforce. One important idea I took from that course was that companies should not simply replace employees as technology advances, but should invest in educating and retraining their employees so they can adapt to an increasingly AI-driven workplace. The environmental concerns discussed in this activity also connected with my previous coursework. We discussed the resources required to operate AI and cloud-computing data centers, including the use of water-based cooling systems to manage the heat generated by computer equipment. Learning about these environmental impacts in both courses reinforced for me that the growth of AI has consequences that need to be considered along with its benefits. Exploring alternatives to ChatGPT and using ChatGPT myself also gave me an opportunity to further evaluate different AI tools and their capabilities. Overall, this activity strengthened my understanding of both the potential benefits and limitations of AI and reinforced the importance of using the technology thoughtfully, ethically, and with human judgment rather than relying on it without critical thinking.
